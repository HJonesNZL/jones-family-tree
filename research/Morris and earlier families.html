Airlie and her children

Airlie May Morris was born on 8 March 1895. An exact-date search of her birth registration returns that day and names her parents Margaret Helen Mary and George Moodie. On 16 December 1926 she married Theodore Warren Jones at the Cathedral of St. Luke, Ancon. The family certificate is signed “Jas. Craik Morris”, Bishop of the Panama Canal Zone. The newspaper describes the ceremony and her mother attending the reception. The surname shared by the bishop and bride does not establish kinship. Certificate · 1926 wedding report · Birth entry

Warren Theodore Jones was born on 31 December 1927 according to his NZ death registration. The Panama newspaper named him and Betty together as the captain’s children, and identified their maternal grandmother as Mrs George Morris. The dates mean Warren died on his 94th birthday, 31 December 2021. His birthplace remains open. Death entry · Family clipping · 11 January 1946 issue · Death notice

Elizabeth Airlie “Betty” Jones can be traced from a hospital notice to a married surname. The July 1929 announcement places Captain and Mrs Theodore Warren Jones’s newborn daughter at Panama Hospital on 17 July. A July 1944 report names Betty at her fifteenth birthday supper in her parents’ home. A January 1952 announcement identifies the parents at Paihia and announces a Jones–Wilson engagement. The death registration for Elizabeth Airlie Wilson gives the identical 17 July 1929 birth date. Together these records explain both the nickname and the year’s difference in the family sheet. 1929 birth notice · 1944 birthday · 1952 engagement · 2006 death registration

Airlie’s death illustrates the difference between event and registration year. The index returns her death on an exact search for 21 December 1977 under reference 1978/26930. It gives her birth as 8 March 1893. Her own 1895 registration takes precedence. Theodore’s 1960 death entry records age 74, also slightly at odds with his 1887 Greenwich birth registration. George Moodie Morris and Margaret Helen Mary Dymock

George Moodie Morris was born 1 December 1861 to Arthur William and Jessie. He and Margaret Helen Mary “Daisy” Dymock married at Manor Place, Dunedin, on 21 September 1892. The marriage announcement calls George his father’s second son and Daisy her father’s eldest daughter. The church index records her age as 22. These identifiers fit the pair named on Airlie’s birth. Don Ferguson describes George as a stock agent and identifies him among the executors of his parents’ estates. The civil death entries give George’s age as 67 in 1929 and Margaret’s as 93 in 1963. The Dymock and Bell branch

Airlie May Morris → Margaret Helen Mary “Daisy” Dymock → William Dymock and Mary Eliza Bell → Edward Bell. Mary Eliza’s own 1868 marriage notice names Edward as her father. The evidence identifying her as Daisy’s mother is corroborated by the birth index, family notices and probate records. The 1870 birth index omits the child’s forename.

William Dymock (c.1834–1911)

On 25 July 1868, William Dymock, an accountant with the Bank of Australasia in Sydney, married Mary Eliza Bell. The Rev. Dr. Lang officiated. The announcement identifies her as the eldest daughter of Edward Bell, civil engineer. The notice appeared on 27 August. Kiama Independent, p. 2 · Saved issue page

The NSW marriage index records William Dymock and Mary E. Bell, Sydney, 701/1868. The full marriage return and the Rev. John Dunmore Lang’s Scots Church register are leads for the couple’s birthplaces and parents. A signed January 1866 bill of exchange in the Macarthur papers already places William at the Bank of Australasia in Sydney. NSW marriage index · Scots Church marriages, Mitchell Library A 2234 · 1866 bill of exchange, p. 356

In January 1860 William took a 99-year lease on nearly seven acres of North Shore land from John Cooper. He built a dwelling on the property, known as Wallaringa, sublet it to solicitor Staunton Spain in 1863 and assigned the lease to Spain in 1872. North Sydney Council’s history cites the original deed, Old System Deed Book 84 No. 215, and distinguishes the site from the later Nutcote house. Nutcote conservation plan, pp. 8–9

By 1870 he was managing the bank’s Christchurch branch. In 1875, after nearly twenty years with the Bank of Australasia, he left to manage the National Bank in Dunedin. The bank promoted him to chief inspector in 1881 and moved its New Zealand head office to Dunedin. The bank’s staff marked his retirement from its general management with a New Zealand silver inkstand inscribed at Dunedin in December 1893. Christchurch bank notice · 1875 farewell · 1881 promotion report · Head office history · Retirement report

William lived at 36 Iverna Gardens, Kensington, and died on 18 December 1911 at 50 Beaumont Street, St Marylebone. Probate was granted in London on 19 September 1912 to his widow Mary Eliza Dymock. The London correspondent’s notice, printed in March 1912, calls him the former Dunedin general manager, says he had moved back to Britain about fourteen years earlier, and names his four surviving children by their adult names or titles. These include Mrs George Morris of Napier, Russell Dymock of Wellington, Mrs Robert Watson of Farnham, and an unmarried Miss Dymock at Iverna Gardens. William’s birthplace and parents remain open. Probate calendar, p. 109 · 1912 obituary

The original GRO death index records William Dymock aged 77 in the Marylebone district in October–December 1911, volume 1a, page 634. That district and quarter match his probate entry. The recorded age suggests birth around 1834. Original GRO death index via FreeBMD, printed p. 216

Mary Eliza Bell (died 1923) and her children

The New Zealand birth index gives William and Mary Eliza Dymock as the parents of a child whose forename was unrecorded, reg. 1870/29446. The register returns 6 July 1870. A Christchurch notice records a daughter of the banker and his wife born that day. Daisy’s 1892 marriage notice names William as her father and the church entry says she was 22. William’s obituary later names Mrs George Morris as his daughter. Taken together, the records strongly support Mary Eliza as Daisy’s mother and make 6 July 1870 the likely birth date. Daisy’s full marriage registration, 1892/3884, could independently name her mother. Civil index · Christchurch birth notice transcription · 1892 marriage notice · Knox Church marriage index

Mary Eliza died on 4 December 1923 at The Bourne Ridge, Farnham, Surrey. Her probate, granted 2 July 1924, names solicitor William Scott Hart and widow Alice Esther Isa Watson. Alice’s wedding report names William as her father and Jessie as her sister. William’s obituary names both Daisy and Mrs Watson among his children, resolving their shared father despite the two wedding reports each calling its bride his eldest daughter. The 1903 bridesmaids and relatives also included Alice’s cousins Miss Duguid and Dorothy Dymock, and her aunt Mrs Cathcart Wason. The identity of Jessie with Violet Jessie Mina Dymock (1875/8688) remains probable. Probate calendar, p. 151 · Alice’s wedding, Evening Star · NZ historical register, Edward Russell McLean 1872/28690 and Violet Jessie Mina 1875/8688

The civil death index gives Mary E. Dymock’s age as 85, in the same district and quarter as the probate death. That implies a birth around 1838, a few years later than the estimate from the reported 1851 census, where her age was 16. A baptism or birth record is needed to resolve this. GRO death index, Farnham 2a 189 · 1851 census lead

Daisy’s eldest brother, William John Lindsay Dymock

A New Zealand family notice identifies William John Lindsay Dymock as the eldest son of William Dymock, formerly of the National Bank, and gives his death from fever in Singapore on 6 December 1900. This accounts for his absence from his father’s list of surviving children. As Lindsay Dymock, he was a 22-year-old clerk who had lived in Dunedin for 16 years when he filed to marry Annie Mary Beal on 18 July 1891. The civil index matches the marriage under 1891/2643. Annie’s death notice names Lindsay as her husband and gives her death at Hobart on 8 September 1892. His age fits a birth shortly after William and Mary Eliza married, and the original birth entry would establish his mother directly. Edward Bell (1812–1875)

Mary Eliza’s marriage notice identifies her as Edward Bell’s daughter. The Joshua passenger list names Edward Bell, M. Ann Bell, Maria Bell, Alice Bell and Mary Eliza Bell among the group that arrived in Sydney from London on 25 January 1856. The linked branches corroborate the identification. Mrs Cathcart Wason, named as Alice Dymock’s aunt in 1903, was born Alice Seymour Bell. Her 1873 marriage notice also names Edward Bell, civil engineer, as her father. The shipping list and engineering biography identify Edward’s wife as Mary (listed as M. Ann). Mary’s maiden name and the original record of her relationship to Mary Eliza remain open. Joshua passenger transcription, NSW State Records NRS 13278, reel 403 · Te Ara on Alice Seymour Bell’s marriage · 1873 marriage notice transcription

The Institution of Civil Engineers’ 1875 obituary gives Edward’s birth as 4 October 1812 in Hackney. He apprenticed with John Hague from 1827, worked on London docks and drainage works, travelled to New York for engineering work in 1836, subsequently worked abroad and lectured on machinery at Putney. He became Sydney’s city engineer in February 1856. His work included the Botany pumping station, Crown Street and Paddington reservoirs and the Hyde Park obelisk, built as a sewer vent. He returned to England and died in Lewisham on 5 May 1875. The parish-register leads below identify his likely parents. 1875 obituary reproduced in the engineering heritage nomination · Dictionary of Sydney · Engineering Heritage Australia, pp. 10–14

Edward, Charles and John Bell

The Sydney Morning Herald death notice in April 1869 identifies Charles Bell MP, who died in Richmond, Surrey, as the brother of Sydney city engineer Edward Bell and of John Bell. The Empire identifies John as manager at Circular Quay. Its account also traces Charles’s work for Thomson, Bonar & Co. in St Petersburg. Parliamentary records place Charles in the City of London seat and his parliamentary biography names Thomas Bell as his father. That strengthens the identification of Edward with the Hackney Bell family. The baptism and will remain important for directly confirming the parent links. Sydney Morning Herald death notice, p. 1 · Empire obituary, p. 3 · Historic Hansard · Parliamentary biography

The Bells in Hackney and Mary Ann Owen

A reported transcription of the St John at Hackney baptism register on 5 April 1815 names Edward as a son of Thomas Bell, a merchant of Well Street, and Anna Maria. Richard and Alfred are reported in the same group. The civic biography of Richard Bell independently cites the same parish register, The London Archives P79/JN1/29, for Richard’s baptism on that date. It identifies the parents as Thomas and Anna Maria and gives Anna Maria’s father as John Russell, an engraver of Plough Court, Fetter Lane. Thomas traded in coal, served as a lieutenant in the Honourable Artillery Company and died on 23 November 1826. The National Archives catalogues Anna Maria’s 1834 will as PROB 11/1839/259. The baptism account is a strong match to the city engineer, with the original entry still to be inspected. A published transcription gives Edward’s birth as 3 October 1812, one day earlier than the engineering obituary. Edward’s parish-register transcription · Richard Bell’s civic biography and source notes · Anna Maria Bell’s will catalogue

Researchers report that Edward Bell and Mary Ann Owen married on 14 August 1834 in St Pancras. The 1851 household transcription places Mary and her eldest daughter Mary E., aged 16, with other children in Wales while Edward was working abroad. The children in that household include Sancton, a distinctive name that helps identify the same family in the 1856 shipping list. The 1868 wedding notice already establishes that the younger Mary Eliza was Edward’s eldest daughter. The 1834 register and the daughter’s baptism would directly establish the mother’s maiden name for this branch. Reported marriage and census findings · Joshua passenger list transcription

The Russell engraver and a Whitechapel lead

Richard Bell’s civic biography names Anna Maria’s father as the engraver John Russell of Fetter Lane. Pam Griffiths’s study of his apprentice Alexander Findlay transcribes an 1803 indenture to John Russell, engraver and Goldsmiths’ Company member, and places the same engraver in Fetter Lane earlier. The study describes his father as John Russell, a gunlock maker of Great Minories. A Whitechapel register transcription records a baptism on 5 May 1751 to John and Judith Russell of Goodman’s Yard. A reported Fleet entry identifies Judith as Judith Wild. The original baptism, Fleet marriage and senior John’s probate administration are the records to compare before extending the family further. Arthur William Morris and Jessie Moodie

George’s father Arthur William Morris came from Irvine, Ayrshire. He owned the barque Dunedin with his brother James. The outward voyage began in 1855 and reached Otago in January 1856. Arthur kept a journal from the age of nineteen. Hocken Collections lists it as PC-0434, and the Alexander Turnbull Library holds extracts about the voyage in MS-Papers-6986. He later managed the Otago and Southland Investment Company and served as a director of the Union Steam Ship Company. He died in 1910 aged 84. His exact December 1825 birth day differs between his own journal (25th) and Ferguson’s reading of the parish register (26th). Hocken Collections guide · Turnbull Library catalogue · Voyage reconstruction with journal extracts · Birth sources

Jessie Moodie, also recorded as Janet, had been in Dunedin just seven days when the 1857 intention to marry was filed. Arthur, a merchant, reported two years’ residence. The notice gave their ages as 31 and 23 and spelt her surname Mudie. Their civil marriage registration uses Moodie. The National Library identifies her as the daughter of George Moodie and Janet Hutton and gives the marriage date as 9 December 1857. Jessie died in 1910 aged 76. Intention to marry, Otago no. 30 · Civil marriage · National Library

The First Church register transcript records Arthur and Jessie’s marriage on 9 December 1857, and the communicant roll lists Arthur in Dunedin in December 1856. Arthur represented Peninsula on the Otago Provincial Council from 1863 to 1867. His death notice gives 12 May 1910, in his 85th year. Jessie died at Wairangi, Littlebourne, on 1 November 1910. Her own notice calls her Arthur’s widow. First Church marriage register · Communicant roll · Biographical entry · Arthur’s death notice · Jessie’s death notice

Morris, Mories and Osburn

  1. Arthur William Morris is recorded in Ferguson’s reconstruction as the son of Alexander Morris (1777–1854), a shipmaster at Irvine, and Marion Howe (1786–1847). The 1820s Scottish services of heirs are particularly valuable here because Marion appears under her maiden and married names and her husband’s occupation.   2. Alexander is attributed to Fergusson Mories (1747–1790) and Jean Arthur. Their marriage is indexed to Kilwinning, 7 December 1772, parish 599, register 3, page 63, frame 799. Jean’s own proposed parentage remains tentative. Fergusson and his children · Jean and marriage reference
  3. Fergusson is attributed to William Morries and Margaret Osburn, married at Kilwinning on 29 December 1741, parish 599, register 3, page 29, frame 765. How, Hunter, McGowan and Boyd

  1. Marion Howe is attributed to James How (1743–1798) and Margaret Hunter (1749–1796). Their 1775 marriage is reported at Irvine following banns in Stevenston. An 1828 inheritance index calls Marion a granddaughter of Joseph Hunter, shipmaster at Irvine. Check the original retour for 11 November 1828, recorded in December, alongside the 1825 service to her sister Janet. Marion and heir index, volume III, pp. 42 and 55 · National Records of Scotland guide
  2. Margaret’s proposed parents are Joseph Hunter (born 1718) and Margaret McGowan. James’s proposed parents are Robert How, a sailor, and Janet Hunter. James’s cited baptism is Ardrossan, 28 August 1743, parish 576, register 1, page 25, frame 19. Margaret Hunter · James How
  3. Ferguson places Robert Hunter (born approximately 1687) and Mary Boyd (approximately 1681) above Janet Hunter of Irvine. The estimates are leads into the late seventeenth century. Janet’s 1722 entry and Joseph’s 1718 entry are crucial tests of the suggested relationship between the two Hunter branches. Repeated forenames make a further generation particularly uncertain. Moodie, Hutton and Harley

George Moodie worked in the revenue service. Ferguson places his household at Blantyre in 1841 and Campbeltown in 1851 and reports his marriage to Janet Hutton at Alloa in 1833. The records to compare include the Alloa marriage register (465/00 0070 0381), the Saline banns (455/00 0020 0143), and the 1851 household with their daughter Janet. George’s life and record references · Hutton household

George’s reported parents are Thomas Mudie and Mary Gordon Nicoll. Janet Hutton’s reported parents are George Hutton and Janet Harley, whose banns were published in Muckart in 1800. Harley’s reported parents are Andrew Harley and Marjory Beverige, with a Culross birth entry in 1778 (343/00 0020 0523).
