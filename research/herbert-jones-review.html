Sarah Herbert of Olney and the Jamaican Jones line

What identifies Sarah

The family sheet records Sarah Herbert’s baptism on 29 January 1826 in Olney and identifies Mary Ann Herbert, baptised there on 3 August 1823, as her elder sister and a witness at Sarah’s marriage to Archimedas Jones on 7 October 1851. Both spouses gave Dock Street addresses. These specific dates provide a way to test the relationship in three separate parish entries. Buckinghamshire’s catalogue lists the Olney parish registers as CBS PR 159/1/1–24. Olney record guide, p. 96

The photograph records the compiler’s account of the entries. It does not show the entries or name Sarah’s parents. Original sheet

Olney’s available FreeREG transcription ends in 1800. A search there cannot decide either sister’s parentage. The Olney & District Historical Society has a separate, free person search covering its local transcriptions. Its 1851 census pages retain the household, occupation, age, birthplace and street fields. The surviving 1851 index gives a specific candidate family. Richard Herbert, born about 1797, is listed as father of Marey Ann (about 1824), Joseph (about 1834), George (about 1837) and William (about 1840). Marey Ann fits the sister named in Sarah’s wedding account, while Joseph fits the Olney-born man in the Sailors’ Home in 1891. Sarah herself is absent from these Olney results. The two matches make Richard a candidate for her father, pending her baptism or marriage register entry. The earlier research located another Olney-born man beside Archimedes in the 1891 Sailors’ Home transcription, Joseph Hubert, aged 57. The birthplace and shared occupation make him worth investigating. His different recorded surname, age and shared workplace cannot establish that he was Sarah’s brother. How far back the Jones line goes

Joseph Jones is the earliest named person on this branch. The family papers report that Archimedes gave Joseph as his father and described him as a merchant when he married in London in 1851. The family’s estimate of a birth around 1790 remains open. The earliest dated lead for Archimedes is a reported St Elizabeth, Jamaica, baptism on 10 October 1822 giving his birth as 27 September 1815. The civil marriage index lists the couple as Archimedas Jones and Sarah Harbert, Whitechapel district, October–December 1851, volume 2, page 713. The full marriage entry would verify the father’s details. The Jamaican baptism image remains to be checked. Researchers’ account of the marriage and baptism

The 1851 Whitechapel marriage

The surviving family sheet is a typed account dated 6 January 2000. It gives 7 October 1851 at St Mary, Whitechapel, Dock Street for both parties, and William Partridge and Mary Ann Herbert as witnesses. It attributes the father’s name and profession to the certificate. The project papers and Claude’s saved research contain the family account and the civil index reference. The full entry is still needed to check Archimedes’s occupation, both fathers’ details, signatures and any annotations. Family sheet, p. 1

The London Archives catalogue identifies the exact register as P93/MRY1/046, May 1851–September 1853, microfilm X024/103. Ancestry’s public results show Archimedas Jones with Sarah. The scan and full fields require access to the collection. The separate banns book for that period is P93/MRY1/219, microfilm X099/083. It may corroborate names and residence if the marriage was by banns. FreeBMD also places Archimedas Jones in the December 1851 quarter, Whitechapel, volume 2, page 713. The detailed 1851 Aldgate census transcription places Archimedes Jones, unmarried, 36, cook, born in Jamaica, among the residents of the Sailors’ Home, Well Street. The Home is schedule 171, where James Laughton is listed as superintendent. The adjoining Destitute Sailors’ Asylum has its own schedule. Who was Joseph Jones?

Page 1 names Joseph Jones as the merchant father reported on Archimedas’s marriage certificate and questions Joseph’s estimated birth year of 1790. The London census identifies Archimedes as born in Jamaica. Neither source identifies Joseph’s estate, Jamaican parish, wife or parents. A researcher reported that the 1822 St Elizabeth baptism gives 27 September 1815 as the birth date and omits the parents. The original register image still needs inspection. The catalogue distinguishes early parish copy registers from later diocesan transcripts. Family photograph · Reported baptism · Register catalogue

Joseph Jones of Old Heathfield, St Elizabeth. UCL dates his recorded ownership to 1809–1817 and cites the 1817 register at T71/165, folios 530–532. The 1820 almanac identifies the proprietor as deceased in its March 1819 returns, and the 1820 register shows possession for Hyman and Judah Cohen, T71/167, folio 144. Those records establish a man of the right name in the parish. His trade and any relationship to Archimedes remain unverified. An October 1813 Royal Gazette notice signed by John Jones of Heathfield shows why the estate name and surname alone are insufficient to identify an individual. Inigo Joseph Jones, Matilda Jones and Margaret McGill. Descendant accounts propose that Inigo and Matilda were children of a Joseph Jones and Margaret McGill. The 1822 St Elizabeth baptism

The named record is FamilySearch VH6X-71T. The early St Elizabeth register is on film 1368561, digital folder 004620530, with a separate parish index on film 1224314. A researcher who saw the entry reported a birth on 27 September 1815 and a baptism on 10 October 1822, with the historical description “white by law” and no parents listed. The record viewer requires sign-in. The comparison needs the full spread and adjacent page, with particular attention to names, shared residence, marginal notes and changes of heading. Probate and the Old Heathfield estate

The March 1821 and later St Elizabeth almanac returns put Heathfield under Cohens and Co., consistent with the 1820 register after Joseph’s death. A register of people held in slavery records possession at that date. The deed or an estate file is needed to identify the parties and terms. The surviving-original Jamaican wills index and the UK National Archives catalogue searches yielded no matching probate entry. The Jamaican Court of Ordinary enrolled will books and grants of administration are separate sources. The Jamaica Archives inventory and appraisal series 1B/11/3 is a target for 1817–1822. The Island Record Office can check its grantor and grantee indexes for an instrument involving the Jones estate and Hyman or Hymen Cohen, Judah Cohen, or their firm.
