Sarah Herbert of Olney and the Jamaican Jones line

What identifies Sarah

The family sheet records Sarah Herbert’s baptism on 29 January 1826 in Olney and identifies Mary Ann Herbert, baptised there on 3 August 1823, as her elder sister and a witness at Sarah’s marriage to Archimedas Jones on 7 October 1851. Both spouses gave Dock Street addresses. These specific dates provide a way to test the relationship in three separate parish entries. The photograph records the compiler’s account of the entries. It does not show the entries or name Sarah’s parents. Original sheet

Olney’s available FreeREG transcription ends in 1800. A search there cannot decide either sister’s parentage. The Olney & District Historical Society has a separate, free person search covering its local transcriptions. Its 1851 census pages retain the household, occupation, age, birthplace and street fields. The surviving 1851 index gives a specific candidate family. Richard Herbert, born about 1797, is listed as father of Marey Ann (about 1824), Joseph (about 1834), George (about 1837) and William (about 1840). Marey Ann fits the sister named in Sarah’s wedding account, while Joseph fits the Olney-born man in the Sailors’ Home in 1891. Sarah herself is absent from these Olney results. The two matches make Richard a candidate for her father, pending her baptism or marriage register entry. The earlier research located another Olney-born man beside Archimedes in the 1891 Sailors’ Home transcription, Joseph Hubert, aged 57. The birthplace and shared occupation make him worth investigating. His different recorded surname, age and shared workplace cannot establish that he was Sarah’s brother. Look for him in an earlier Olney household, alongside a sister of the right name and age, then compare the parents’ details in the baptism records. How far back the Jones line goes

Joseph Jones is the earliest named person on this branch. The family papers report that Archimedes gave Joseph as his father and described him as a merchant when he married in London in 1851. The family’s estimate of a birth around 1790 remains open. The earliest dated lead for Archimedes is a reported St Elizabeth, Jamaica, baptism on 10 October 1822 giving his birth as 27 September 1815. The original register and the full marriage entry are still needed to check those particulars. Researchers’ account of the marriage and baptism

A transcription of the 1851 census lists a Jamaica-born Archimedes Jones, aged 36, as a cook in Middlesex. The transcription omits the census page and street. The family research places him at the Sailors’ Home, where the 1891 census identifies him as a Jamaican-born steward. The two entries fit the same man across forty years. Royal Museums Greenwich holds the Home’s wages books from 1873 under SAH/54 and directors’ minutes from 1841 under SAH/1. 1851 census transcription · 1891 census transcription · Sailors’ Home archive catalogue

Jones derives from John and occurs in both Welsh and English surname traditions. The surname alone cannot locate Joseph’s family before Jamaica. Society for Name Studies in Britain and Ireland

Joseph Jones and Heathfield

Page 1 names Joseph Jones as the merchant father reported on Archimedas’s marriage certificate and questions Joseph’s estimated birth year of 1790. The London census identifies Archimedes as born in Jamaica. Neither source identifies Joseph’s estate, Jamaican parish, wife or parents. A researcher reported that the 1822 St Elizabeth baptism gives 27 September 1815 as the birth date and omits the parents. The original register image still needs inspection. Claude’s dossier located a Joseph Jones in the Old Heathfield ownership records, and the 1820 almanac identifies a deceased Joseph Jones there. Those records document the proprietor. A will, deed, guardianship, apprenticeship or other record naming Archimedes would connect that separate biography to the family. The baptism’s reported omission of parents cannot be used to infer a father’s prior death. The forum suggestion of Margaret McGill as mother and the possible relationship to Inigo Joseph Jones require their own evidence. Keep these people outside the documented pedigree.
