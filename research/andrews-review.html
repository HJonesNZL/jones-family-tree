Charles Henry Jones and Isabel Andrews

Charles Henry’s career and household

The family papers place Charles Henry’s birth on 31 March 1857 in Whitechapel. His 1920 biographical entry agrees with the year and records private education, fourth place in the 1873 competitive examination, and entry as a third-class clerk in the General Register and Record Office of Shipping and Seamen. At about sixteen he began a career with the office that lasted almost half a century. The family’s 1881 occupation agrees with that early grade. The entry gives his progression to staff clerk in 1891 and assistant to the Registrar General in 1901. It names literature as his recreation. In 1920 it gives his home as 11 Emperor’s Gate and his office at Tower Hill. Who’s Who entry · Family sheet, page 2

The November 1913 trade press names Charles H. Jones as successor to H. N. Malan. It says he had worked in the department forty years and describes his appointment as the first time someone on the office staff had been promoted to its head. The forty years independently fit his 1873 start. The January 1918 honours list records the CBE. In 1921 the Board of Trade named John Blake Harrold MBE as his successor. 1913 appointment · 1918 Gazette · 1921 Board of Trade notice

The 1936 probate calendar records his death on 3 September, address 1c Nevern Square, South Kensington, and administration with will granted to widow Isabel in London on 30 October. Effects were £1,177 11s 4d. Her own entry records death at Wallingford on 5 November 1947, address care of Lady Isabel Margaret Braund at Upton near Didcot, and probate on 16 January 1948 to Dame Isabel Margaret Braund, wife of Sir Henry Benedict Linthwaite Braund. These entries link persons and addresses across the Jones and Braund branches. Charles’s calendar entry · Isabel’s calendar entry

Isabel’s family in the East End

The October–December 1883 Greenwich marriage index, volume 1d page 1480, lists both Isabel Andrews and Charles Henry Jones. It also lists Robert George Andrews and Lucy McQuay. The family sheet supplies the couple, exact date of 25 October, and witness William Andrews. Charles Henry’s contemporary biographical entry independently names his wife Isabel and her father as John Andrews. The index alone cannot pair the four names. Marriage index checked directly · Who’s Who

Isabel Andrews’s birth was indexed in October–December 1861 at Mile End, volume 1c page 541, according to the FreeBMD result preserved in Claude’s session. This fits the nine-year-old daughter in the April 1871 census and the family’s approximate 1862 Stepney birth. The search result supplies no maternal maiden name. A birth registration quarter can fall after the actual birth. Birth index result

I checked the FreeCEN household transcription directly. At 74 Cotton Street, Limehouse, return RG10/553, page 39, schedule 195, John T Andrews, 44, born Shadwell, worked as a gauger in HM Customs. Sarah A Andrews, 38, his wife, was born in St George in the East. The household records these children by relationship to John. John, 16, was a commercial clerk born Ratcliff. William, 13, Kate, 11, Isabel, 9, Arthur, 4, and Frank, 2, were born in Mile End. Annie, eleven months, was born in Limehouse. William, Kate and Isabel were scholars. A widowed aunt Ann Potter, 54, was an annuitant born in Shadwell. Her surname and birthplace offer a way to investigate John’s family, though her listing does not identify her birth surname or which parent connects her. Sarah A is the wife in the household. An 1861 birth entry naming both parents would independently establish maternity and her maiden name. The son William is a plausible candidate for the 1883 witness. The marriage sheet does not specify their relationship. 1871 household

Open candidates

John’s middle initial is T in the census. Expanding it to Thomas remains tentative. An 1853 Lambeth registration for John Thomas Andrews has brides Emma Hall and Jane Hannah Scuddimore Kiddell on the same page. This entry supplies no basis to identify Sarah A. The Sevenoaks death of a John Thomas Andrews, July–September 1893, aged 66, volume 2a page 421, fits the age. The index gives neither family nor occupation. Both items were recovered from Claude’s saved search results. The next discriminating evidence would be Isabel’s 1861 birth entry, the fathers’ occupations in her 1883 marriage register entry, and a customs service or pension record linking the gauger to a later residence. 1853 marriage candidates · National Archives guide to Customs officers
